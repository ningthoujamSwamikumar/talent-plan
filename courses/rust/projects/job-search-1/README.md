# Project: Job Search Sprint

**Phase 11 — Job Search Execution**

This is not a coding project. This is the project where you actually get hired.
Everything you've built over the last 8-12 months comes together here. You will
run a structured, disciplined job search with real applications, real networking,
and real tracking.

The job search is a skill, and like any skill, it gets better with practice. Most
people fail at job searching not because they're unqualified but because they
search randomly, get discouraged by rejections, and give up. This project gives
you a system.

## Deliverables

By the end of this project you will have:

- [ ] A target company list of 30+ companies
- [ ] A polished resume tailored for Rust backend roles
- [ ] 10+ applications sent with customized cover letters/applications
- [ ] An application tracking spreadsheet
- [ ] Active participation in at least 2 Rust communities
- [ ] At least 3 networking conversations with Rust engineers

---

## Part 1: Build Your Target Company List

Create a spreadsheet with these columns:

| Company | Product | How They Use Rust | Remote Policy | Careers URL | Contact | Status | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------------------|---------------|-------------|---------|--------|-------|

**Where to find companies hiring Rust engineers:**

1. **Rust-specific job boards:**
   - [rustjobs.dev](https://rustjobs.dev)
   - [This Week in Rust](https://this-week-in-rust.org/) (jobs section)
   - [filtra.io/rust](https://filtra.io/rust)

2. **General job boards with Rust filter:**
   - LinkedIn: search "Rust engineer" + "remote"
   - Indeed/Glassdoor: "Rust backend engineer"
   - We Work Remotely: search "Rust"
   - Hacker News monthly "Who is hiring" thread: Ctrl+F "Rust"

3. **Company lists:**
   - [Companies using Rust in production](https://www.rust-lang.org/production/users)
   - [Awesome Rust companies](https://github.com/omarabid/rust-companies)
   - Crunchbase: search for companies using Rust in their stack

4. **Conference sponsor pages:**
   - RustConf sponsors
   - EuroRust sponsors
   - These companies are investing in the Rust ecosystem

**Target at least 30 companies.** You won't apply to all of them, but having a
large list prevents you from putting all your eggs in one basket.

## Part 2: Finalize Your Resume

Using the template from bb-career-1, create your final resume:

1. **Start with a master resume** — include everything.
2. **Create a "Rust backend" variant** — emphasize systems, web, and distributed
   work.
3. **Have 2 people review it** — ideally someone who hires engineers and someone
   who's been recently hired. If you don't know anyone, post it (anonymized) on
   r/cscareerquestions or r/rust for feedback.

**Resume checklist:**
- [ ] One page (two pages ONLY if you have 10+ years of experience)
- [ ] No typos (use a spell checker AND have a human read it)
- [ ] Consistent formatting (dates, bullet style, font)
- [ ] Every bullet starts with a strong verb (built, implemented, designed,
      reduced, increased)
- [ ] Every bullet has a result or metric
- [ ] GitHub link is prominent
- [ ] Published crate is mentioned
- [ ] Open source contributions are mentioned with project names
- [ ] No "References available upon request" (obviously they are)
- [ ] PDF format (not Word/Google Doc)

## Part 3: Application Cadence

**Week 1-2: Ramp up**
- Apply to 2-3 roles per week
- These are your "practice" applications — apply to roles you're interested in
  but that aren't your top choices
- Learn what the application process feels like

**Week 3-8: Steady state**
- Apply to 3-5 roles per week
- Customize each application:
  - Adjust your resume summary to mirror the job posting language
  - If there's a cover letter field, write 3-4 sentences about why THIS company
  - Reference their product by name

**Week 9+: Adjust based on results**
- If you're not getting callbacks: ask for resume feedback, broaden your search,
  consider applying to mid-level roles
- If you're getting callbacks but no offers: focus on interview practice
- If you're getting offers: focus on negotiation

**For each application, track:**
- Date applied
- Role title
- Company
- How you found it (job board, referral, direct)
- Status (applied, phone screen scheduled, technical interview, etc.)
- Notes (interviewer names, questions asked, follow-ups needed)
- Next action and date

## Part 4: Active Networking

Networking is not asking strangers for jobs. Networking is becoming a known
member of the community so that when jobs come up, people think of you.

**Weekly activities:**

1. **Answer 2-3 questions per week** on Rust Discord, Stack Overflow, or the
   Rust Users Forum. Be genuinely helpful. Include code examples.

2. **Engage on social media** — comment thoughtfully on Rust-related posts on
   Twitter/X, LinkedIn, or Mastodon. "Great post!" doesn't count. Add value:
   "We tried this approach and found that X was tricky because Y."

3. **Attend one virtual meetup per month** — Rust meetups, local tech meetups,
   or online events. You don't need to present (but you should if you can).

**Networking conversations:**

Reach out to 3 Rust engineers for informational conversations. These are NOT
"can you get me a job?" conversations. They are "I'm building my career in
Rust and I'd love to learn from your experience" conversations.

**How to ask:**
```
Hi [Name],

I've been following your work on [project/blog/talk] and really enjoyed
[specific thing]. I'm building my career as a Rust backend engineer and
would love to hear about your experience at [company/in the Rust ecosystem].

Would you be open to a 20-minute chat? I'm happy to work around your
schedule and time zone.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**During the conversation, ask:**
- How did you get into Rust professionally?
- What does your team look for when hiring Rust engineers?
- What's the most undervalued skill in a Rust engineer?
- What do you wish you knew when you started?

**After the conversation:**
- Send a thank-you note within 24 hours
- Connect on LinkedIn
- Follow up if they mentioned anything you can help with

## Part 5: Handling Rejection

You will be rejected. Probably many times. This is normal and expected.

**The numbers:**
- Average job search for a software engineer: 2-4 months
- Average applications before an offer: 20-50
- Average interview-to-offer conversion rate: 10-25%

**What rejection means:**
- "No response" = your resume didn't match their ATS keywords. Adjust keywords.
- "Rejected after recruiter screen" = the role wasn't a fit (level, tech stack,
  timing). Ask for feedback if possible.
- "Rejected after technical interview" = specific skill gap or interview
  performance. Ask what you could improve.
- "Rejected after final round" = you were close. This is painful but means
  your skills are nearly there. Ask for specific feedback.

**What to do after rejection:**
1. Ask for feedback (many companies won't give it, but some will)
2. Write down what you learned
3. Apply to 2 more companies the same day (momentum matters)
4. Don't take it personally — hiring is noisy and imperfect

## Part 6: Evaluating Offers

When you get an offer (and you will), use this framework:

| Factor | Weight | Questions to Ask Yourself |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| Compensation | High | Is it market rate? (Check levels.fyi) |
| Technical work | High | Will you grow? Is the tech stack interesting? |
| Team | High | Did you like the people you interviewed with? |
| Remote policy | Medium | Truly remote or "remote but come to the office quarterly"? |
| Growth | Medium | Is there a path to senior/staff? |
| Company stage | Medium | Startup risk vs. big company stability? |
| Benefits | Medium | Health, PTO, equipment stipend, learning budget? |
| Equity | Variable | At $0 for startups. Meaningful for public companies. |

**Never accept immediately.** Always say: "Thank you, I'm very excited about
this opportunity. I'd like to take [2-3 days / a week] to review the details.
Can you send the offer in writing?"

Then: negotiate (see bb-career-3), compare offers if you have multiple, and
make a decision.

## Success Criteria

- Target list has 30+ companies
- Resume is finalized and reviewed by at least 1 other person
- 10+ applications sent with tracking spreadsheet
- Active in at least 2 Rust communities (consistent weekly participation)
- 3 networking conversations completed
- You have a system for handling rejection and maintaining momentum

## Timeline

This project runs alongside your job search. Expected timeline:

- **Month 1**: Build target list, finalize resume, start applying (5-10
  applications), begin community participation
- **Month 2**: Steady applications (15-20 total), first interviews, adjust
  approach based on feedback
- **Month 3-4**: Continue applying, interviewing, and iterating. By now you
  should be getting interviews regularly
- **Month 4-6**: Offers and negotiation. If not getting offers by month 4,
  reassess: are you targeting the right roles? Is your interview performance
  the bottleneck?

The search ends when you accept an offer. Celebrate. Then go back to building.
